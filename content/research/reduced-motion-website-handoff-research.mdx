# Reduced Motion Evidence in Website Design Handoffs

**Published: September 10, 2026**

This WebsiteDesignOutsource.com study asks how a team can document reduced-motion behavior without claiming that one preference query resolves every motion-related risk. Sources were reviewed September 10, 2026.

## Research question

What inventory and test evidence can show how a website responds to motion preferences and WCAG requirements?

## Methodology and scope

We reviewed WCAG 2.2 criteria and guidance concerning animation and moving content, the Media Queries specification, and CSS guidance for `prefers-reduced-motion`. Scope includes CSS and script-driven motion in web interfaces. Video captioning and hardware display settings are excluded.

## Evidence synthesis

The media feature communicates a user's preference for less nonessential motion. WCAG addresses motion triggered by interaction and provides pause, stop, or hide requirements for certain moving content. These are related controls, not substitutes. A preference response can reduce a transition while a separate pause control remains necessary for continuing movement.

## Practical interpretation

Inference: teams should maintain a motion inventory with trigger, duration, repetition, purpose, default behavior, reduced alternative, and owner. Verification should run each component with both preference states, reload the page, navigate within the application, and confirm that disabled reveals do not hide content. Record motion that remains and the reason.

## Inference boundaries and limitations

The preference does not identify a medical condition or prescribe one universal design response. Browser support and application architecture affect when changes take effect. This method does not establish full WCAG conformance or measure an individual's physical response.
