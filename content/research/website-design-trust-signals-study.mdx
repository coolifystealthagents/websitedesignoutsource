# Trust Signals on Website Design Service Pages

Trust is not a decorative layer added with badges. On a service page, it comes from clear claims, identifiable ownership, relevant evidence, and a route that lets a visitor check the details. An outsourced website team can improve the presentation, but the company must supply and approve the facts.

## Types of evidence

Separate first-party facts, third-party standards, client evidence, and illustrative examples. A standard can explain a requirement, but it cannot prove that a particular project met it. A testimonial can describe one experience, but it cannot establish a universal result. A screenshot can show an interface, but it may omit context. Label the evidence type beside the claim.

Schema.org Article and Organization vocabularies can describe published information, yet structured data does not make an unsupported claim true. Google’s structured-data guidance similarly treats markup as a representation of visible page content. The visible page and its metadata should agree.

## Review questions

Ask whether the source is named, whether the date matters, whether the wording matches the evidence, and whether a visitor can understand the limitation. Avoid absolute words unless the source supports them. Use a description of the work or acceptance condition when no guarantee exists.

Accessibility is part of trust. A person who cannot reach a proof link by keyboard or identify a heading has less access to the same evidence. Check contrast, focus, link purpose, alternative text, and heading order using WCAG as the reference.

## Limits

Trust signals do not predict conversion or remove the need for direct questions. A page review can find unsupported wording and confusing evidence, but only the business can confirm operational facts. Keep the original source and approval date with the content record.

## Methodology

This synthesis uses W3C, Google Search Central, Schema.org, and usability evaluation guidance. It does not assess a specific company claim.

## Key Takeaways

- Identify what each proof item can and cannot establish.
- Keep visible claims consistent with structured metadata.
- Treat access to evidence as part of the user experience.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a logo a trust signal?

It can identify an organization, but clear service information and evidence are still necessary.

### Can a team publish an unverified testimonial?

No. Use an approved real source or omit the claim.
