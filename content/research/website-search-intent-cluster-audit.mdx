# Search Intent Cluster Audits for Website Research

A research library becomes easier to maintain when each article has a distinct question, audience, evidence set, and place in the internal-link structure. Topic volume alone is not a reason to publish.

## Key Stats

- WCAG 2.2 has 13 guidelines (W3C)
- Schema.org defines Article as a content type (Schema.org)
- Sitemaps protocol uses a loc element for each URL entry (Sitemaps protocol)

## Key Takeaways

- Separate topic, intent, and format before drafting.
- Compare proposed slugs and titles with both research and blog content.
- Link to the next useful decision, not merely a related keyword.

## Methodology

Official search, link, schema, sitemap, and accessibility documentation supplies the technical context. The clustering model is a practical editorial method and does not claim ranking or traffic results.

## Cluster audit

For each candidate record the question, reader, decision stage, evidence gap, proposed title, canonical slug, parent cluster, existing overlaps, and two contextual links. Reject a candidate when it only changes wording without changing the question or evidence.

## Internal-link review

Read each link in context and confirm its destination adds a next step. Check that link text describes the destination, the target exists, and the relationship remains valid after a slug change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can two articles target similar words?

They can, but only when the questions, readers, and evidence are materially different. Otherwise combine or defer the candidate.

### When should links be checked?

At drafting, after slug changes, and in the final content validation pass.
