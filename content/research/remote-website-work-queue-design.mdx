# Designing a Remote Website Work Queue

An outsourced website queue should make readiness, ownership, priority, and acceptance visible. Without those fields, a team can be busy while the owner cannot tell what is actually ready to approve.

## Useful queue fields

Capture request, goal, audience, route, source material, dependencies, acceptance criteria, owner, priority, due window, status, evidence link, and escalation rule. Limit work in progress according to the team’s actual review capacity.

## Methodology

This note combines service-design, agile, and web QA guidance. It offers a process model rather than a universal productivity benchmark.

## Key Stats

- The Scrum Guide defines a Product Backlog as an ordered list of work needed to improve a product (Scrum.org)
- Kanban guidance emphasizes visualizing work and limiting work in progress (Kanban University)

## Key Takeaways

- Make a work item ready before assigning it.
- Give every item one accountable owner.
- Treat review capacity as part of throughput.

## Frequently asked questions

### What makes an item ready?

The goal, scope, inputs, owner, and acceptance evidence are clear enough for work to begin without guessing.

### Who sets priority?

The accountable site or product owner, using impact, risk, and timing.
