# Accessibility Test Plans for Outsourced Website Teams

An outsourced accessibility review works best when it turns standards into repeatable checks, clear evidence, and an owner-approved exception log.

## Key Stats

- WCAG 2.2 is organized around 4 principles (W3C WCAG 2.2)
- WCAG conformance levels are A, AA, and AAA (W3C WCAG 2.2)
- WCAG 2.2 includes 13 guidelines (W3C WCAG 2.2)

## Key Takeaways

- Test keyboard, focus, names, contrast, structure, and zoom as separate controls.
- Attach a route, step, expected result, observed result, and screenshot to each finding.
- Treat automated output as a signal that needs human review.

## Methodology

This synthesis compares the normative WCAG 2.2 material with WAI testing guidance, HTML semantics, ARIA practices, and common browser documentation. Counts are taken from the cited standards. The workflow recommendations are operational interpretations for an outsourced delivery team.

## Test plan design

Start with representative templates and high-value journeys. Record the browser, assistive technology, viewport, route, and date. A vendor can execute the checks, but the company should retain the exception decisions and accessibility risk acceptance.

## Evidence that travels

Use one finding per defect. Include the success criterion, exact element, reproduction steps, expected behavior, observed behavior, severity rationale, and retest result. A short evidence pack makes review faster than a long undifferentiated audit.

## Consolidated statistics

The article uses 4 WCAG principles, 3 conformance levels, and 13 guidelines. These are structural facts about WCAG 2.2, not estimates of a website's accessibility.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an automated scanner prove conformance?

No. It can identify useful signals, while keyboard, content, focus, and assistive technology behavior still require human review.

### Who owns exceptions?

The company owner should approve accepted risk. The outsourced team should document the evidence and proposed remediation.
