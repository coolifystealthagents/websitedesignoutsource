# Caption Delivery Evidence for Website Video

**Published: September 10, 2026**

This WebsiteDesignOutsource.com review examines evidence for delivering captioned prerecorded video on a website. Sources were reviewed September 10, 2026.

## Research question

Which source, file, player, and human-review checks support a defensible caption handoff for prerecorded web video?

## Methodology and scope

We compared WCAG 2.2, W3C media accessibility guidance, the WebVTT specification, and HTML media and track requirements. Scope covers prerecorded video with synchronized audio embedded in HTML. Live captions, broadcast regulation, and native applications are outside scope.

## Evidence synthesis

WCAG requires captions for prerecorded synchronized media at Level A, subject to stated exceptions. WebVTT defines a timed-text format, while HTML connects text tracks to media and exposes track kind and language. Technical delivery does not establish caption accuracy. Timing, speaker identification, meaningful non-speech audio, and correspondence to the final edit require content review.

## Practical interpretation

Inference: the handoff should preserve the approved transcript, final media checksum or version, caption file, language metadata, player configuration, and a full-playback review record. Test caption selection with keyboard and at narrow widths. Compare several timestamps, including overlapping speakers and meaningful sounds, then review the entire program rather than treating samples as proof of completeness.

## Inference boundaries and limitations

This method cannot guarantee equal support across every player, browser, and assistive technology. A transcript may help some visitors but does not automatically replace synchronized captions. The review does not cover audio description, legal obligations by jurisdiction, or translation quality.
