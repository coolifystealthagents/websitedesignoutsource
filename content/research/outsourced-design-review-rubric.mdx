# An Outsourced Website Design Review Rubric

Subjective review becomes more useful when the reviewer names the criterion and evidence. A rubric should guide judgment without pretending that design quality is reducible to one score.

## Four review lenses

Review intent and hierarchy, task clarity, accessibility, and implementation readiness. Ask what the user is trying to do, what the page asks them to notice, how the interface behaves, and whether the design explains its states.

## Make feedback actionable

Each comment should identify location, observed issue, impact, recommendation, priority, and owner. Separate blocking defects from preferences so a distributed team can resolve the important work first.

## Methodology

This synthesis combines usability heuristics, accessibility standards, and government design-system practices. It is a review aid, not a substitute for user research.

## Key Stats

- Nielsen Norman Group describes 10 general usability heuristics (NN/g)
- WCAG 2.2 has three conformance levels (W3C)

## Key Takeaways

- Review against agreed criteria, not personal taste alone.
- Tie feedback to user impact and evidence.
- Distinguish blockers from refinements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a rubric produce a pass or fail?

Use a decision only where acceptance requires one. Preserve the underlying comments and evidence.

### Who should resolve preference disputes?

The accountable product or site owner, using the agreed audience and goal as the tie-breaker.
