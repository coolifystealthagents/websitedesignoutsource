# Analytics and Consent Handoffs for Outsourced Websites

Analytics implementation is a product handoff as much as a technical task. Clear event names, consent behavior, and ownership prevent a dashboard from becoming an untrusted record of the site.

## Key Stats

- GDPR defines 6 lawful bases for processing (EUR-Lex GDPR Article 6)
- Google Consent Mode documents 2 consent mode versions (Google)
- GA4 recommends event parameters for additional context (Google Analytics)

## Key Takeaways

- Define events with purpose, trigger, parameters, and consent state.
- Test denied, granted, and changed consent paths independently.
- Keep measurement ownership with the company, including account access.

## Methodology

This synthesis compares GDPR provisions with Google Analytics and Consent Mode documentation, ICO guidance, and browser privacy standards. Legal application depends on jurisdiction and facts; the operational controls here are not legal advice.

## Event contract

For every event, record the business question, event name, trigger, parameters, examples, and excluded data. A vendor may implement the contract, but the company should approve what is collected and why.

## Consent test matrix

Test first visit, rejection, partial consent, later change, and withdrawal. Confirm tags, storage, network requests, and reporting behavior. Save timestamps and screenshots with the build identifier.

## Consolidated statistics

The article identifies 6 GDPR lawful bases and 2 documented Consent Mode versions. These counts describe source frameworks and do not determine whether a specific implementation is compliant.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an analytics vendor decide the consent design?

The vendor can propose an implementation, but the company should approve purposes, data, consent behavior, and retention with appropriate legal review.

### Should event names change whenever a page changes?

No. Events should remain tied to stable user actions and business questions, with versioned changes documented.
