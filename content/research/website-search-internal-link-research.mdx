# Internal Linking Research for Outsourced Website Content

Internal links are part of information architecture, not decoration added at the end of writing. A source-led linking plan gives each article a useful next step and a reviewable relationship to the site's services.

## Key Stats

- Google Search's SEO starter guide identifies internal links as a core discovery mechanism (Google)
- HTML defines 2 link relationship directions: source and destination (WHATWG)
- WCAG 2.2 has 4 principles (W3C)

## Key Takeaways

- Link where the destination adds context to the current task.
- Use descriptive anchor text and verify every target.
- Review orphan pages, loops, and links that cross content families incorrectly.

## Methodology

This synthesis uses Google Search documentation, the HTML specification, WAI link guidance, and WCAG 2.2. The 2 link directions are a simplified model for this workflow, not a count of every HTML relationship.

## Link map

Build a map of source page, destination page, purpose, anchor text, family, and review date. Research pages should point to related Research work unless a Blog link is deliberately useful and clearly classified.

## QA checks

Check status, canonical destination, label clarity, mobile placement, keyboard focus, and whether the destination answers the promise of the anchor. Remove links that exist only to increase a count.

## Consolidated statistics

The article cites internal links as a Google discovery mechanism, models 2 link directions from HTML, and uses 4 WCAG principles. These are framework facts, not SEO performance forecasts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many internal links should an article contain?

There is no useful universal quota. Add links when they clarify the reader's next step, then verify the destination and ownership.

### Should Research and Blog links share one inventory?

They can share a site map, but family labels and counts should remain separate so reporting does not confuse the two publishing routines.
