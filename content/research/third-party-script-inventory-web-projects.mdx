# Third Party Script Inventories for Outsourced Web Projects

**Published: August 31, 2026**

This WebsiteDesignOutsource.com research note examines third party script inventory as an acceptance and ownership problem. It synthesizes primary standards and official guidance verified on August 31, 2026. A checklist alone does not prove compliance or project success.

## Research question

What information and evidence should a client require when an outsourced team changes this area? Record the purpose, implementation location, accountable owner, test method, decision state, dependencies, and final handoff location.

## Evidence model

Distinguish normative requirements, platform guidance, project decisions, and observations. A citation explains the basis of a requirement but does not prove a page passes. Proof comes from testing the implemented page with repeatable steps.

- Record each requirement and authoritative basis.
- Link designs to implemented locations.
- Test normal, error, empty, loading, and constrained states.
- Name the owner authorized to accept residual risk.
- Retest after material changes.

## Handoff controls

Include the current specification, implementation notes, dependencies, licenses or account ownership, exceptions, unresolved risks, and verification procedure. Store the record in a company-controlled system and ensure the client can operate, update, and reverse the implementation without relying on one vendor account.

## Review interpretation

Apply only requirements relevant to the interface and technology. When sources do not cover a project-specific choice, record the decision and rationale. Inference: treating third party script inventory as a lifecycle record gives design, build, and release reviewers one stable object to inspect.

## Key Stats

- WCAG 2.2 organizes its success criteria under four accessibility principles (W3C).
- The NIST Secure Software Development Framework groups practices into four practice groups (NIST).

## Key Takeaways

- Treat third party script inventory as an owned lifecycle record.
- Connect acceptance statements to implementation evidence.
- Preserve exceptions with the final handoff.
- Recheck the record after relevant releases.

## Methodology

Sources were selected for authority and direct relevance, favoring standards bodies, government guidance, and official platform documentation. Links were verified on August 31, 2026. Quantitative statements are limited to structural facts described by the named source. Recommendations are a synthesis.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does citing a standard prove a pass?

No. A reviewer must test the real implementation and retain evidence.

### Who owns final acceptance?

The client names an accountable owner. The vendor supplies implementation detail, evidence, and fixes.

### When should the record be reviewed?

Before implementation, at acceptance, and after changes to the relevant component, dependency, content model, or delivery path.
