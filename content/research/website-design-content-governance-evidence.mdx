# Content Governance Evidence for Outsourced Websites

Content governance is the set of decisions that keeps a page accurate after publication. It is different from a style guide. A style guide can describe voice and formatting; governance identifies who owns a claim, how a change is requested, what evidence supports it, and when the page should be reviewed. This distinction matters when an external team contributes to a site that remains the company’s responsibility.

## Record ownership by content type

Assign an owner for service facts, legal text, accessibility statements, technical instructions, and editorial interpretation. One person may own several categories, but the record should still name the category. A designer should not silently approve a legal claim, and a writer should not infer a product promise from a visual mockup. The owner field is a control against accidental authority.

Google Search Central recommends keeping information accurate and useful for people. W3C guidance adds accessibility requirements for content structure and relationships. Together, they support a review record that includes the claim, source, route, date checked, reviewer, and disposition. The record should preserve rejected suggestions when the reason will help future reviewers.

## Change evidence

Each requested change should identify the current text, proposed text, reason, affected route, and acceptance condition. If the change alters an internal link or canonical, include those fields too. A broad request such as “make it clearer” needs a testable interpretation before production. The owner can choose a wording preference, but the record should distinguish preference from evidence.

## Review intervals and limits

Review timing depends on the content’s risk and rate of change. A legal notice, service scope, and evergreen explanatory page do not share one interval. Avoid inventing a universal schedule. Use a review date as a prompt, then confirm whether the source remains current. Governance can reduce ambiguity, but it cannot prove that a statement is true without a suitable source.

## Methodology

This article synthesizes W3C accessibility guidance, Google Search Central, Schema.org, and NIST risk concepts. It is a practical ownership model, not legal advice or a promise of search performance.

## Key Takeaways

- Name an owner for each high-risk content category.
- Preserve source, date, and decision evidence with every material change.
- Separate style preference from a factual or accessibility requirement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does governance require a large committee?

No. It requires clear ownership and a repeatable decision record. A small team can use the same fields with fewer reviewers.

### Should every sentence have a citation?

No. Claims that affect trust, scope, compliance, or decisions need traceable support. Ordinary transitions do not.
