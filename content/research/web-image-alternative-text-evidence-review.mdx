# Web Image Alternative Text Evidence Review

**Published: September 11, 2026**

This study reviews evidence for choosing and testing text alternatives according to image purpose. Sources were observed September 11, 2026.

## Methodology

We used a structured documentary review of WCAG 2.2, WAI image tutorials, HTML, accessibility mappings, and government guidance. Concepts were coded by image function and converted into reviewable production decisions. No automated alt-text generator was evaluated.

## Scope, population, and observation window

The population is visitors encountering informative, functional, decorative, complex, or text-bearing images, including people using screen readers or browsing without loaded images. Scope covers HTML images and linked images on content pages. Video description, canvas, and social-platform previews are excluded. The review reflects guidance accessible on September 11, 2026.

## Evidence synthesis

The evidence makes context central. Informative images need concise equivalents, functional images need the action or destination, decorative images generally need an empty alternative, and complex graphics need a nearby fuller explanation. A filename or generic object label rarely captures purpose.

## Inference and causal boundaries

Inference: teams should approve image purpose before drafting text, then inspect final HTML and the page with images unavailable. Meeting a text-alternative requirement does not establish that prose is accurate, concise, or useful. Evidence here cannot support causal claims about engagement, conversion, or search ranking.

## Limitations

This review did not test comprehension, multilingual wording, AI-generated descriptions, SVG accessibility, or a specific screen-reader and browser matrix. Judgment remains necessary because identical assets can serve different purposes on different pages.
