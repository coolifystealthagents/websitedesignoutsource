# How to Brief Responsive Breakpoints for Outsourced Website Design

"Make it responsive" does not tell a designer what should change. A better brief describes where layout decisions stop working and what the page should do when space becomes tight.

## Describe behavior, not phones

Name the relationship between columns, navigation, images, text, and actions. Say when a two-column section should stack, whether the image moves after the text, and how a long label should wrap. Device labels can hide the real constraint.

## Use representative content

Review the longest heading, a paragraph with realistic length, the full navigation, and the largest expected image. Short sample content often makes a layout look stable when the approved copy will not fit.

## Test the awkward states

Ask the outsourced website design team to show narrow width, large text, zoom, a long button label, and a missing or slow-loading image where those states matter. Record what is allowed to move and what must remain available.

## Keep the brief inspectable

Attach the page route, content source, expected order, and review owner. The final handoff should include the tested widths and any intentional exception. This gives a later maintainer a reason for the responsive rule rather than a list of unexplained breakpoints.


## Choose breakpoints from content failure

Do not begin with device names. Resize the real navigation, headline, table, form, and card content until a task becomes unclear or a control becomes unusable; those failures are evidence for a layout change. Record the transition, expected behavior, and test content so the outsourced website design team can reproduce the decision at intermediate widths.
