# What to Include in a Cookie Consent Design Brief

Consent interfaces combine content, interaction, privacy decisions, and accessibility. An outsourced design team needs a brief that explains the choices the company has approved before it designs the banner or preference view.

## List the purposes

Name each purpose in language a visitor can understand and distinguish necessary operation from optional measurement or personalization. Coordinate the final wording with the company’s privacy owner.

## Define the available choices

Record which choices are offered, how they are revisited, and what happens after each selection. Do not make a visual preference look like the only possible action.

## Design for return visits

Explain where a visitor can reopen preferences and how the current state is communicated. The route should remain usable without blocking the main content unnecessarily.

## Check accessibility and states

Review keyboard order, focus visibility, labels, contrast, small screens, loading, and error states. Save the approved copy with the design record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a designer decide the consent policy?

No. The company’s privacy owner decides the policy. The designer translates approved choices into a clear and usable interface.

### Should the banner contain every legal detail?

It should provide clear essential information and a route to fuller details. The company’s legal and privacy reviewers should approve the final content.
