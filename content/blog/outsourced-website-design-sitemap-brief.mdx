# A Sitemap Brief for an Outsourced Website Design Project

Inventory current and proposed routes, page purpose, owner, status, and relationships. Mark pages to keep, combine, redirect, or remove and explain each decision.

## Make the decision visible

Separate the public navigation from the full inventory. Not every useful page belongs in the top navigation, but every retained route needs a reason and an owner.

## Review the real page

Ask the design team to show the sitemap beside the navigation and page templates. Check labels, depth, breadcrumbs, footer paths, and the route plan together.

## Keep the handoff useful

A clear sitemap reduces surprise during redesign and gives an outsourced team a reliable structure to reflect in prototypes and handoff notes.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the brief include?

Include the visitor, page purpose, approved evidence, required states, owner, and the decision the reviewer must make.

### Who should approve the design?

Name one person inside the company who can approve the content, page behavior, and final handoff.
