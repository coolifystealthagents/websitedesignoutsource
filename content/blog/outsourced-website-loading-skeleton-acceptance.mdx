## Define the acceptance boundary

At checkpoint 1, the client records a representative slow response and the final content geometry. The outsourced team can prepare implementation evidence and explain observed behavior, while the frontend lead retains decisions about scope, risk acceptance, policy, and release. The checkpoint receives the stable identifier WD13-B-outsourced-website-loading-skeleton-acceptance-1; screenshots without the route, state, input, and build identifier are supporting material rather than a complete result.

For define the acceptance boundary, the reviewer exercises throttled navigation from an empty cache and preserves the initial condition, exact action, observed result, expected result, browser or device context, and retest result. Particular attention goes to layout movement, stale placeholders, and controls that appear ready before they work. If the approved brief does not resolve an exception, the team leaves the state unchanged where practical, links the evidence, identifies the affected visitor journey, and routes the decision to the frontend lead. That boundary prevents a delivery vendor from silently converting uncertainty into a product, accessibility, privacy, security, or operational commitment.

The define the acceptance boundary checkpoint closes only when a second person can repeat the observation from the record. For loading skeleton behavior, the handoff should show which condition passed, which remained unsupported, and what would trigger another review after release. The client should reject labels such as “looks fine” or “works on mobile” because they omit the fixture and decision rule. Instead, the record names the tested route, selected content, environmental constraint, evidence location, decision owner, due point, and rollback or containment action. This makes later maintenance comparable to the accepted release rather than dependent on memory.

No. The delivery team can document loading skeleton behavior, propose a correction, and repeat the test. The frontend lead accepts residual risk or names another accountable decision maker.

No. The record must preserve the route, input or state, environment, expected result, observed result, and repeatable retest instructions.
