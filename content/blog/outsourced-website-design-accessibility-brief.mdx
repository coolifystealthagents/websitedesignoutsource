# An Accessibility Brief for Outsourced Website Design

Accessibility is easier to review when it appears beside the page requirements. Give the outsourced designer clear behavior to build and clear evidence to provide.

## Name the requirements

Record keyboard access, focus visibility, heading order, labels, alternative text, contrast, zoom, and error handling where they apply. Link each requirement to a page element instead of listing standards without context.

## Use content that can be tested

Provide real headings, long labels, link text, and form messages. A short sample can hide a wrapping or focus problem. Ask who owns the wording when an accessible label needs editorial approval.

## Review interaction states

Check default, focus, hover where relevant, error, disabled, and completed states. Include the route a keyboard user takes and what a screen reader should understand from the structure.

## Keep evidence with the handoff

Record the page, test method, finding, and disposition. A note that says "accessible" is not enough to guide a later correction.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an accessibility brief remove the need for testing?

No. It tells the team what to build and inspect. Testing still needs to happen on the actual page.

### Who should review accessible copy?

The content owner should review meaning and language. The design or technical reviewer should check interaction and structure.
