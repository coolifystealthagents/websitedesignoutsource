# Define a Legal Page Change Workflow for Website Teams

**Published: September 2, 2026**

Privacy, terms, cancellation, and other policy pages combine legal decisions with routine web publishing. The client and qualified advisers own the substance; an outsourced website team needs a precise, auditable implementation request.

## Package approved content

Provide the final copy, policy type, locale, approver, approval date, intended effective date, canonical route, page title, and required cross-site links. Mark superseded drafts clearly. Do not ask a designer to decide what the policy should promise.

## Map every affected surface

- Global header or footer navigation.
- Forms and consent notices.
- Checkout or account flows.
- Downloadable copies and language variants.
- Structured metadata and internal search.

## Preserve change evidence

Record the previous version, approved replacement, release identifier, publication time, and routes checked. If historical versions must remain available, define their location, access rules, and labels. An archive requirement should come from the responsible client owner.

## Verify the public result

Confirm the canonical route returns successfully, the visible effective date matches the approved copy, and links reach the intended version. Check representative global components and user journeys. Escalate any mismatch in wording rather than editing legal text during QA.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the outsourced team rewrite policy text for clarity?

Only through the client review path with appropriate legal approval. Production staff should not improvise policy meaning.

### Should old policy pages redirect?

The responsible client owner should define archive and redirect rules based on the organization’s requirements.
