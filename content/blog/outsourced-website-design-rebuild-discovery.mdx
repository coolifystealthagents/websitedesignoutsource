# Discovery Questions Before an Outsourced Website Rebuild

An outsourced rebuild should begin with what the existing site does, not only how old it looks. Discovery questions help the team protect useful routes and identify decisions that the visual brief cannot answer.

## Ask what must survive

List pages, forms, downloads, integrations, redirects, analytics needs, policy links, and source assets that cannot disappear without an owner decision. Include pages that are hard to find but still serve a known audience.

## Ask who can decide

Name the owners of content, brand, access, technical constraints, and final acceptance. If a page has competing owners, record the conflict before design begins. The outsourced website design team should not resolve a governance problem by choosing the loudest comment.

## Ask what evidence exists

Collect the current route list, page titles, approved brand rules, content inventory, known defects, and source locations. Mark assumptions clearly. Discovery is useful when it exposes missing evidence instead of filling gaps with invented detail.

## Turn answers into the brief

Separate preserved behavior, planned changes, open questions, and out-of-scope work. Give the first page a clear acceptance condition. That makes the rebuild reviewable before a large set of templates and routes is committed.
