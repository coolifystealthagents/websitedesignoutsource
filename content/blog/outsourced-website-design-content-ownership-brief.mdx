# Write a Content Ownership Brief for Outsourced Website Design

Design decisions become easier when the team knows who owns the words, images, claims, and final approval. A content ownership brief turns those assumptions into a usable handoff record.

## Divide content by decision

List page copy, headlines, calls to action, images, downloadable files, metadata, testimonials, legal text, and structured fields. For each item, name the person who supplies it and the person who approves its public use.

## Clarify what the design team may change

State whether the outsourced team can edit grammar, shorten labels, crop images, reorder sections, or suggest new copy. Keep factual claims and regulated language with the company’s named subject owner.

## Set a version rule

Identify the approved source, revision date, and change summary. If copy changes after layout review, show the affected routes and send only the relevant decisions back through review.

## Cover reusable content

Explain which blocks, labels, images, and metadata can be reused and which require route-specific approval. Reuse should preserve meaning, not create nearly identical pages with different headings.

## Close with acceptance evidence

The final record should show the approved content version, rendered route, links, image notes, metadata, and owner decision. Keep open questions separate from accepted content so later updates start from a known state.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the outsourced team write final copy?

It can draft or edit when that responsibility is agreed. The company should still approve factual claims, brand language, legal text, and the version that becomes public.

### What if no one owns an asset?

Pause that asset’s use and name an owner before approval. Do not rely on an unattributed image, claim, or download simply because it appeared in an earlier draft.
