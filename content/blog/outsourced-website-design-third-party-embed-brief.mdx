# Prepare a Third-Party Embed Brief for Outsourced Web Design

Maps, schedulers, media players, and other embeds can change the behavior of a page outside the design file. A brief makes those dependencies visible before an outsourced team commits to a layout.

## Name the purpose and owner

Explain what the embed helps a visitor do and who owns its account, content, permissions, and support. The company should retain control of the service relationship.

## Record the loading behavior

Describe dimensions, loading, consent, failure, mobile, keyboard, and fallback states. Ask whether the page remains useful when the provider is unavailable.

## Check the surrounding content

Introduce the embed with enough context and provide an alternative route when the embedded experience cannot serve every visitor. Do not let the provider’s frame become the only explanation.

## Rehearse updates

Document how provider changes are noticed, reviewed, and rolled back or replaced. Include the dependency in future route reviews.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should every embed be kept?

No. Keep it when it serves a clear visitor need and its privacy, access, performance, and fallback requirements are acceptable.

### Who approves provider changes?

The company owner of the dependency should approve changes, with design and accessibility review when the route behavior changes.
