# Create a Content Expiry Register for an Outsourced Website

**Published: September 1, 2026**

Campaigns, event pages, offers, staff profiles, and policy notices can become misleading when nobody owns their review date. A content expiry register turns future cleanup into planned operational work.

## Record what makes content stale

For each item, capture its canonical URL, content type, business owner, publication date, review date, expiry condition, dependencies, intended action, and approval route. Distinguish a review date from an automatic removal date so useful evergreen material is not deleted by mistake.

## Plan the end state before publishing

- Choose update, archive, redirect, or removal criteria.
- Identify incoming links and navigation placements.
- Preserve required records outside the public site.
- Schedule owner reminders with enough review time.

## Manage risk and ownership

Do not let a vendor decide alone whether regulated, contractual, or historically important content can disappear. A redirect should serve a relevant successor, not merely hide an expired page.

## Define acceptance

Review the register at handoff and confirm every dated item has an active client owner. Include the procedure for updating internal links, metadata, sitemap entries, and redirects.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who should own this record?

A named client owner should approve business rules and access. The outsourced team maintains implementation detail and supplies repeatable evidence.

### When should the team review it?

Review it before implementation, at acceptance, and whenever a related service, policy, account, or production component changes.

### Does a template prove the work is complete?

No. The completed record must match the implemented website and be supported by checks that another reviewer can repeat.
