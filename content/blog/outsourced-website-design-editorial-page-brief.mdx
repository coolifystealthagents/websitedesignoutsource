# The Editorial Page Brief for an Outsourced Website Design Team

State the visitor, page job, primary proof, required sections, internal links, and action. Keep the brief focused on what the page must accomplish rather than prescribing every visual detail.

## Make the decision visible

Add a “not on this page” note for tempting but secondary information. This protects the page from becoming a collection of every stakeholder request.

## Review the real page

Review the brief with the person who owns the source material and the person who owns the business decision. Resolve conflicts before layout work expands.

## Keep the handoff useful

The external team gets a stable target, while your company keeps control over the content purpose and the approval criteria.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the brief include?

Include the visitor, page purpose, approved evidence, required states, owner, and the decision the reviewer must make.

### Who should approve the design?

Name one person inside the company who can approve the content, page behavior, and final handoff.
