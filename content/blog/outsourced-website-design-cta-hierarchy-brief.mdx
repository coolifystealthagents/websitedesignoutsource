# CTA Hierarchy for an Outsourced Website Design Brief

Pages become hard to review when every link is treated as a call to action. Before design starts, decide which action is primary, which links support it, and what a visitor should expect after selecting it.

## Describe the decision

Write the visitor's next decision in plain language. A service page may ask for a conversation, while a guide may point to a related explanation. The label and surrounding copy should match that intent. Do not choose a stronger-looking button to compensate for an unclear offer.

## Limit competing actions

List required secondary links and explain why each one belongs. If a link is useful but not urgent, the design can give it lower visual weight. This is a content decision first, then a layout decision for the outsourced website design team.

## Include destination behavior

Record the destination route, whether it opens in the same window, and what confirmation or next page the visitor should see. The review should cover the link itself, its focus state, its mobile position, and its text when read out of context.

## Approve from the page goal

The owner approves the hierarchy against the page purpose. The designer should call out conflicts, such as a legal link that must remain easy to find or a form action that needs more explanation. Keep those decisions in the handoff record.


## Tie each action to a decision

For a service page, the primary action may ask for a conversation while a secondary action lets a cautious visitor inspect proof or requirements. Write what the reader knows before each action, what the click promises, and what happens when the reader is not ready. Review the first screen, proof section, destination, and footer together so repeated actions reinforce one promise instead of introducing competing ones.
