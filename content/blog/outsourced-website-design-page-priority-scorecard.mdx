# A Page Priority Scorecard for Outsourced Website Design

An outsourced website design team needs a page order, not a long list of URLs. A simple scorecard helps the owner decide which page should receive research, design, copy review, and QA first.

## Score the job, not the page count

Give each candidate page a short note for visitor importance, business consequence, evidence quality, and change risk. A page used in a current campaign may outrank a popular but stable page. A page with unclear ownership may need discovery before design begins.

Use plain labels such as high, medium, and low. Avoid pretending that a total score is a measurement of revenue. The score only makes the reasoning visible to the external team.

## Add a readiness check

Mark whether the page has approved copy, usable images, a known call to action, and a named reviewer. If two high-priority pages are equally important, start with the one that has fewer unresolved inputs. It gives the team a useful test of the brief without hiding a content problem inside the first draft.

## Keep exceptions visible

Some pages must move early because another page depends on them. Record those dependencies beside the score. Also note pages that must keep their URL, form behavior, or legal wording. This prevents a design queue from quietly becoming a migration plan.

## What to hand off

Send the scorecard with the page list, decision owner, source links, and the acceptance condition for the first page. Ask the outsourced website design team to repeat the chosen order and identify any missing input before work starts.
