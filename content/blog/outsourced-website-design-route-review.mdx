# Review Routes Before an Outsourced Website Design Change

A visual redesign can have a quiet effect on URLs. Review routes before layouts change so an outsourced website designer knows which addresses are stable and which require a documented decision.

## List the routes in scope

Record current URL, page purpose, owner, status, and destination after the change. Mark routes that appear in navigation, search results, campaigns, or useful external links.

## Decide each change explicitly

Keep, change, combine, redirect, or retire each route. Record the reason and the person who approves it. Do not let a missing page in a mockup make the decision by accident.

## Check links and measurements

Review internal links, canonical information, analytics references, and any external communication that depends on the address. Technical owners should confirm implementation details.

## Test the final route

Open the published path, follow key links, and record the result. Keep unresolved redirects with an owner until they are checked.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should route review happen before design?

Yes. It gives the designer a stable page list and exposes dependencies before a visual decision is treated as final.

### Who approves a route change?

Name the owner of the page or site structure. Implementation teams can advise, but should not infer the business decision.
