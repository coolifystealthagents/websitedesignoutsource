# Make a Color and Contrast Brief for an Outsourced Website

A palette is more useful when it explains meaning and behavior, not only hex values. A short brief lets an outsourced team design consistent states while keeping accessibility visible.

## Name semantic roles

Describe roles such as page background, body text, link, action, warning, and success. Keep role names stable even if the visual values change.

## Include interaction states

Show default, hover, focus, active, disabled, visited, and error behavior where applicable. A color that works in one state may not communicate in another.

## Check non-color cues

Do not make color the only way to identify a status, error, or required action. Pair it with text, shape, position, or another perceivable cue.

## Verify real combinations

Test text, icons, borders, controls, overlays, and images against their actual backgrounds. Keep the evidence with the approved palette record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a brand color automatically accessible?

No. Accessibility depends on the specific foreground, background, text size, and state combination.

### Who approves exceptions?

The company brand owner and accessibility reviewer should approve any exception, with the reason and affected routes recorded.
