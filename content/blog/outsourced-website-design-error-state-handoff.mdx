# Hand Off Website Error States Without Guesswork

Error states shape trust when something goes wrong. A clear handoff helps an outsourced website design team design honest messages and recovery routes instead of hiding failures behind generic copy.

## Classify the failure

Record whether the problem comes from input, access, a missing route, a temporary service issue, or an unknown condition. The classification guides both the wording and the next action.

## Write for recovery

Tell the visitor what happened, what they can do now, and where to get help if the action fails again. Avoid exposing technical details that do not help recovery.

## Preserve entered information

For forms and multi-step flows, state which information remains available after an error and how the visitor can correct it. This should be reviewed with the product or service owner.

## Test real states

Ask for evidence of invalid input, timeouts, unavailable content, permissions, and narrow layouts. Confirm that status is announced appropriately and focus lands where the visitor can continue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should all errors use the same message?

No. A visitor needs different guidance for a missing field, an unavailable service, and an unknown failure.

### Who owns operational error wording?

The company owner of the service should approve the meaning and support route. The design team can shape the presentation.
