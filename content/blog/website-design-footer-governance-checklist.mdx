# Use a Footer Governance Checklist for Website Design

**Published: September 2, 2026**

A footer appears across many pages, so one stale link or unsupported claim can spread site-wide. Its repeated position makes it a component with content, legal, navigation, accessibility, and operational owners.

## Inventory every footer element

List navigation groups, legal links, company details, contact methods, social destinations, newsletter forms, trust marks, language controls, and copyright treatment. For each item, record its destination or source, owner, update trigger, and required states.

## Review the component as content

- Use labels that explain the destination.
- Confirm keyboard order and visible focus.
- Check headings and landmarks in page context.
- Test wrapping at supported widths.
- Verify errors and success states for any form.

## Prevent silent drift

Choose one maintained data source where practical. When legal pages, offices, service names, or social accounts change, include the footer in the change request. Avoid copying footer markup into separate templates that can diverge.

## Define acceptance evidence

Sample representative page types, not only the homepage. Record broken-link results, responsive screenshots or observations, keyboard checks, and content-owner approval. Recheck production because global caching or an incomplete deployment can leave different versions live.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who owns footer copy?

Assign owners by content type. Legal, contact, marketing, and navigation details may need different approval.

### How often should links be checked?

Set a routine based on change frequency and recheck after any global navigation release.
