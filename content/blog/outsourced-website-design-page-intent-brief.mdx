# How a Page-Intent Brief Improves Outsourced Website Design

Write one sentence beginning with “After this page, the visitor can…” and one sentence explaining what evidence makes that possible. Use them to order the page sections.

## Make the decision visible

Reject extra sections that do not help the stated decision. A page can be complete without answering every adjacent question.

## Review the real page

Compare the first screen, headings, proof, and action with the intent statement. If the page asks for an action before establishing relevance, revise the order.

## Keep the handoff useful

A shared intent statement gives your design partner a way to make tradeoffs and gives reviewers a common standard for acceptance.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the brief include?

Include the visitor, page purpose, approved evidence, required states, owner, and the decision the reviewer must make.

### Who should approve the design?

Name one person inside the company who can approve the content, page behavior, and final handoff.
