# Review Pagination and Canonical Signals Before Launch

**Published: October 13, 2026**

## Define the acceptance boundary

Review Pagination and Canonical Signals Before Launch begins with pagination and canonical signals as a testable visitor outcome for visitors and crawlers traversing multi-page listings, not a visual preference that can be approved from a static mockup. At checkpoint 1, the client records the page series, filter policy, canonical target, and discoverable links. The outsourced team can prepare implementation evidence and explain observed behavior, while the SEO owner retains decisions about scope, risk acceptance, policy, and release. The checkpoint receives the stable identifier WD13-B-outsourced-website-pagination-canonical-review-1; screenshots without the route, state, input, and build identifier are supporting material rather than a complete result.

For define the acceptance boundary, the reviewer exercises first, middle, last, filtered, empty, and out-of-range pages and preserves the initial condition, exact action, observed result, expected result, browser or device context, and retest result. Particular attention goes to collapsing useful pages into one canonical or creating duplicate parameter combinations. If the approved brief does not resolve an exception, the team leaves the state unchanged where practical, links the evidence, identifies the affected visitor journey, and routes the decision to the SEO owner. That boundary prevents a delivery vendor from silently converting uncertainty into a product, accessibility, privacy, security, or operational commitment.

The define the acceptance boundary checkpoint closes only when a second person can repeat the observation from the record. For pagination and canonical signals, the handoff should show which condition passed, which remained unsupported, and what would trigger another review after release. The client should reject labels such as “looks fine” or “works on mobile” because they omit the fixture and decision rule. Instead, the record names the tested route, selected content, environmental constraint, evidence location, decision owner, due point, and rollback or containment action. This makes later maintenance comparable to the accepted release rather than dependent on memory.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the design team approve its own exception?

No. The delivery team can document pagination and canonical signals, propose a correction, and repeat the test. The SEO owner accepts residual risk or names another accountable decision maker.

### Is one screenshot enough evidence?

No. The record must preserve the route, input or state, environment, expected result, observed result, and repeatable retest instructions.
