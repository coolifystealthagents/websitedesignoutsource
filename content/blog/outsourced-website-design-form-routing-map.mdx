# Build a Form Routing Map for an Outsourced Website Project

**Published: September 1, 2026**

A polished form can still fail the business if a submission reaches the wrong inbox or disappears between systems. A form routing map gives the client and outsourced team one shared record of where each submission goes and how delivery is proved.

## Map every handoff after submit

List the public form, its purpose, required fields, validation behavior, destination system, notification recipients, confirmation message, retention rule, and accountable owner. Include conditional routes such as location, service type, or language. Document what happens when an integration is unavailable instead of assuming the platform will recover.

## Test with traceable submissions

- Use a unique test value so each submission can be followed.
- Confirm the visitor sees the correct success or error state.
- Verify the record arrives in the destination with intact fields.
- Check notifications without exposing form data to unnecessary recipients.

## Manage risk and ownership

Do not treat an on-screen success message as proof of delivery. Keep credentials outside the map, restrict access to personal data, and assign a client owner who can change recipients after handoff.

## Define acceptance

Acceptance evidence should connect a test submission to its destination record, notification, consent text, and recovery procedure. Recheck routing after domain, mailbox, CRM, or automation changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who should own this record?

A named client owner should approve business rules and access. The outsourced team maintains implementation detail and supplies repeatable evidence.

### When should the team review it?

Review it before implementation, at acceptance, and whenever a related service, policy, account, or production component changes.

### Does a template prove the work is complete?

No. The completed record must match the implemented website and be supported by checks that another reviewer can repeat.
