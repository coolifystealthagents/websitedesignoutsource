# Write a Search Intent Brief for Outsourced Website Design

Search language can reveal the job a visitor expects a page to do. A search intent brief turns that signal into design guidance without treating a keyword as a substitute for useful content.

## State the visitor question

Write the underlying question in plain language and name the likely stage of the decision. This gives the design team a reason for the page structure.

## Define the minimum answer

List the information that must be easy to find, the evidence that adds confidence, and the next related question. Keep the page useful even when a visitor reads only the opening section.

## Match structure to intent

Choose headings, comparisons, examples, or explanations based on the question. Do not add sections simply to create a longer page.

## Review the result as a visitor

Ask whether the page answers the stated question quickly, supports a sensible next step, and avoids claims the company cannot substantiate. Revisit the brief when search intent changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is search volume required for a design brief?

No. The brief can begin with the visitor question, existing page evidence, and business relevance. Quantitative research may add context later.

### Should the phrase appear in every heading?

No. Use natural language that helps people understand the page and its sections.
