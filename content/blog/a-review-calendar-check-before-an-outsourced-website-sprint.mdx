## Why the review calendar comes before the design work

An offshore design sprint can stall before the first screen is drawn because the client reviewer is already booked during the proposed feedback window. A calendar check makes that constraint visible while the schedule is still negotiable. It is not a project plan and it does not replace the brief. The check simply confirms who will review the work, when they are available, and how quickly a decision can travel back to the designer.

## Name the reviewer and the backup

A named reviewer is useful only when that person has both the authority and the time to answer. Record the primary reviewer, a backup, and the deputy who can approve a page when both are away. Ask each person to confirm the review window in writing rather than assuming a meeting invitation is enough. If the client cannot name a backup, mark the risk and ask the account owner to resolve it before the sprint starts.

## Check the real calendar, not the optimistic one

Teams often plan against a clean calendar and discover that the reviewer is travelling, closing a quarter, or covering another launch. Compare the proposed review dates with the client's published holidays, known travel, and internal approval cycles. Leave at least one working day between the design handoff and the review meeting so the client can read the material. A short calendar check catches collisions that a status report will not show until the work is already late.

## Set the feedback window in working hours

State the feedback window in the client's time zone and the design team's time zone. A window that ends on the client's Friday evening may leave the designer unable to start before Monday. Define what counts as a complete review response, including page-level comments and an explicit approval or rejection. If the window closes without a response, record the silence and escalate to the named backup instead of guessing.

## Protect the designer's focus time

Reviews consume design time even when the comments are small. Block a correction window after each review so the designer is not expected to switch between three clients in one afternoon. The queue owner should avoid booking a new kickoff against the same correction window. When the calendar is tight, reduce the number of pages in the review rather than asking the designer to work without a protected slot.

## Record the calendar decision with the brief

Attach the confirmed review dates, reviewer names, time zones, and escalation path to the project brief. If a date changes, update the brief and notify the designer through the agreed channel. A calendar note in a private message is easy to lose and hard to audit later. The written decision gives the next coordinator a starting point when the original reviewer leaves the project.

## Recheck the calendar before the sprint opens

Run the check again on the day before the sprint starts. Confirm that the reviewer still holds the slot and that the brief, content, and brand assets are ready for review. If a dependency is missing, decide whether to delay the sprint or reduce its scope. Starting without a confirmed slot spends the first revision round on scheduling instead of design.
