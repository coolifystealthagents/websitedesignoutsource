# Use a Page Goal Matrix With an Outsourced Web Design Team

An outsourced website project can accumulate attractive pages that do not help visitors decide. A page goal matrix makes the purpose of every route explicit before design review begins.

## Record one primary job

Write the main question a page answers or the action it enables. Secondary needs can be noted, but they should not compete with the main purpose.

## Add audience and evidence

Identify who needs the page and what would make the claim credible to that person. Evidence may include a process explanation, a clear example, a qualification, or a useful next step.

## Define the success signal

Choose an observable outcome such as reaching a related page, understanding a service, or completing an agreed contact path. Avoid vague goals such as “make it engaging.”

## Review the matrix with the layout

Ask the design team to show where the goal, evidence, and next step appear. If a page needs several competing destinations, consider whether it should be split or supported by stronger navigation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a page have more than one audience?

It can, but record the primary audience first. If their needs diverge sharply, separate routes may produce a clearer experience.

### Who maintains the matrix?

The company should own it as a content and product record. The outsourced team can update design implications as the site evolves.
