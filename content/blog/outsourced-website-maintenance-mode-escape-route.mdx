# Plan a Maintenance Mode Escape Route Before Release

**Published: October 13, 2026**

## Define the acceptance boundary

Plan a Maintenance Mode Escape Route Before Release begins with maintenance mode and escape routes as a testable visitor outcome for visitors and operators during a planned outage, not a visual preference that can be approved from a static mockup. At checkpoint 1, the client records the activation authority, allowed routes, status link, and rollback condition. The outsourced team can prepare implementation evidence and explain observed behavior, while the operations owner retains decisions about scope, risk acceptance, policy, and release. The checkpoint receives the stable identifier WD13-B-outsourced-website-maintenance-mode-escape-route-1; screenshots without the route, state, input, and build identifier are supporting material rather than a complete result.

For define the acceptance boundary, the reviewer exercises activation, health check, administrator access, cache purge, and rollback and preserves the initial condition, exact action, observed result, expected result, browser or device context, and retest result. Particular attention goes to locking operators out, hiding incident updates, or caching the maintenance response. If the approved brief does not resolve an exception, the team leaves the state unchanged where practical, links the evidence, identifies the affected visitor journey, and routes the decision to the operations owner. That boundary prevents a delivery vendor from silently converting uncertainty into a product, accessibility, privacy, security, or operational commitment.

The define the acceptance boundary checkpoint closes only when a second person can repeat the observation from the record. For maintenance mode and escape routes, the handoff should show which condition passed, which remained unsupported, and what would trigger another review after release. The client should reject labels such as “looks fine” or “works on mobile” because they omit the fixture and decision rule. Instead, the record names the tested route, selected content, environmental constraint, evidence location, decision owner, due point, and rollback or containment action. This makes later maintenance comparable to the accepted release rather than dependent on memory.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the design team approve its own exception?

No. The delivery team can document maintenance mode and escape routes, propose a correction, and repeat the test. The operations owner accepts residual risk or names another accountable decision maker.

### Is one screenshot enough evidence?

No. The record must preserve the route, input or state, environment, expected result, observed result, and repeatable retest instructions.
