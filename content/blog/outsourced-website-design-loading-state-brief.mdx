# What to Put in a Loading-State Brief for Outsourced Website Design

Name the operation, the expected wait, the stable context to preserve, and the message that explains what is happening. Keep navigation and cancellation behavior explicit.

## Make the decision visible

Do not use a spinner as the only explanation when a task may take long enough to create doubt. A short status message can reduce repeated clicks.

## Review the real page

Include slow-network, empty, success, and failure states in the same review. Check motion preferences and ensure a keyboard user is not trapped while content loads.

## Keep the handoff useful

These details let the external team design a calm transition instead of leaving a blank region that looks broken.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the brief include?

Include the visitor, page purpose, approved evidence, required states, owner, and the decision the reviewer must make.

### Who should approve the design?

Name one person inside the company who can approve the content, page behavior, and final handoff.
