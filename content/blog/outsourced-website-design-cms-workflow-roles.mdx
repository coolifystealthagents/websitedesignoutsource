# Assign CMS Workflow Roles Before Outsourced Publishing Starts

**Published: September 4, 2026**

Giving a production partner an editor account answers how they log in, not what they may publish. A CMS workflow needs named responsibilities for content, design, legal checks, scheduling, and urgent corrections.

## Map actions to roles

List the actions the CMS allows: draft, edit, upload media, alter navigation, approve, schedule, publish, unpublish, and restore. Give each role only the actions it needs. Keep account ownership and final access decisions with the client.

## Use a visible publishing path

- The producer assembles the page from an approved brief.
- The subject owner checks meaning and current facts.
- The design reviewer checks layout and responsive behavior.
- The publisher confirms metadata, timing, and final approval.
- The operations owner handles rollback or urgent correction.

Avoid approvals buried in chat. Put the decision beside the page record, with the version and date being approved.

## Plan for absence and urgency

Name a backup for every approval that can block a launch. Define what qualifies for emergency publication, who may authorize it, and what retrospective review follows. Emergency access should not become the routine workflow.

## Audit the first two weeks

Review a small sample of pages for skipped states, excessive permissions, late feedback, and undocumented edits. Adjust the workflow based on observed friction rather than adding roles preemptively.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the agency hold the only publisher account?

No. The client should retain administrative control and a tested route to publish or recover content.

### Can one person draft and publish?

For low-risk updates it may be reasonable, but record the exception and use an independent check for consequential pages.
