# Map Content Approval for an Outsourced Website Design Project

Design and copy often move together, but they do not always have the same approver. A content approval map helps an outsourced team know whose decision is needed and when a page is ready.

## Separate kinds of approval

Distinguish factual, legal, brand, accessibility, and layout approval. A single person may hold several roles, but the decisions should remain visible.

## Define the evidence

Record what a reviewer receives: source copy, rendered route, link list, asset record, or change summary. Reviewers should be able to judge the decision from the same evidence.

## Set handoff conditions

State what must be complete before design begins, before copy is placed, and before the route is accepted. Keep unresolved questions separate from accepted decisions.

## Preserve the record

Store the approved version, owner, date, and meaningful changes together. If copy changes after layout approval, send the route back through the affected checks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can one person approve everything?

Sometimes. Even then, record which kinds of decisions that person is making so gaps are not hidden by a generic approval label.

### What happens when reviewers disagree?

Return to the page goal and evidence, then name the owner who has authority to decide. Keep the unresolved question explicit until it is settled.
