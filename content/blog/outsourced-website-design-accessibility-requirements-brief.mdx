# An Accessibility Requirements Brief for Outsourced Website Design

An accessibility requirements brief makes inclusive behavior visible before visual decisions harden. It connects user needs and standards to concrete page behavior that an outsourced design partner can review.

## Name the critical interactions

List menus, dialogs, forms, media, and navigation patterns that need keyboard, focus, and assistive technology review. Link each requirement to the relevant pattern or standard.

## Include content requirements

Specify heading structure, link purpose, labels, alternatives for meaningful images, and readable error messages. These are design inputs, not polishing tasks.

## Review with evidence

Ask for a usable route and inspect focus, errors, zoom, and narrow layouts. Record findings with enough detail to reproduce them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What belongs in the brief?

Include the visitor task, content and evidence constraints, review conditions, and the owner for the decision. Keep assumptions separate from confirmed facts.

### How should an external team be reviewed?

Review the working route and representative states against the brief. Record a specific change and its reason rather than giving only a general visual reaction.
